Smart is the front door for Uniswap v4 hooks on Robinhood Chain. There are 29,000+ hooks deployed on the chain, and almost none of them have a place where people can actually trade their pools. Smart gives every hook pool a trading page.

What you can do

  • See which hook pools are trading right now, ranked by live swaps.
  • Open any pool by token address, pool id or hook address.
  • Read exactly what the pool's hook is allowed to do, decoded from its address.
  • Buy or sell in one click, from your own wallet, through the official Uniswap Universal Router.
  • Share a direct link to any pool.

How it's built

Smart is a single web page. It reads the chain directly and builds standard Uniswap transactions in your browser. There is no Smart server between you and the chain, and no Smart contract ever holds your funds.

New here? Start with Trading, then read Hook permissions before you trade an unknown hook.

Find a pool

Pick one from Hook pools trading right now on the home page, or paste a token address, pool id or hook address into the search box. For a token, Smart looks up its ETH pool on-chain.

Buy or sell

Choose Buy (ETH → token) or Sell (token → ETH), enter an amount and check the estimate. The minimum received is the estimate minus your slippage setting.

Before you sign

Every swap is simulated against the live chain first. If it would fail, or a hook would take more than your slippage allows, you see the reason and nothing is sent.

Fees

Smart charges 0.25%, shown in the quote before you swap. The pool's own fee and the hook's behaviour come on top, as on any Uniswap interface. See Swap fee.

Uniswap's router moves tokens through Permit2. The first time you sell a token, your wallet asks for two standard approvals:

  • Token → Permit2: lets Permit2 move this token for you.
  • Permit2 → Universal Router: lets the router use that permission. It expires after 30 days.

After that, selling the same token is a single transaction. ETH never needs an approval.

Every pool page shows the price, recent swaps, the hook's permissions, a risk label and a verdict.

Risk labels

  • High · can change swap amounts: the hook may change what you pay or receive in a swap.
  • High · can change LP amounts: the hook may change what liquidity providers deposit or get back.
  • Medium · can gate withdrawals: the hook decides whether liquidity can be removed.
  • Medium · runs on swaps: the hook runs code before or after every swap and may set the fee.
  • Low: none of the above.

Verdict

Real hook means at least 50 swaps across the hook's pools in the last 6 hours, or 5+ swaps in this pool in the last ~10 minutes. Otherwise the hook is marked barely used. A hook nobody trades is a warning sign on its own.

Share links

Every pool has a link: smartrobin.xyz/?pool=0x…. Hook teams can put it in their bio.

In Uniswap v4, what a hook may do is written into the last 14 bits of its address. Smart decodes them for every pool. Permissions marked ! can affect your money directly.

A permission says what a hook can do, not what it does. A hook with return-delta rights may be perfectly fair, or may not. Check its swaps and its team.

$SMART launched fairly on Pons, Robinhood Chain: 1,000,000,000 supply, no team mint, no presale.

What gives it a reason to exist

  • Swap fees buy it back. 0.25% of every swap through Smart buys $SMART and burns it. More hook trading means more buybacks.
  • Featured slots burn it. Hook projects burn $SMART to be featured at the top of Smart.

Both only reduce supply. Track every burn live on the Token page.

Smart takes 0.25% of each swap, in ETH, inside the same transaction as your swap. It's never charged separately.

  • Buys: the router first sends 0.25% of your ETH to the fee recipient (command TRANSFER), then swaps the rest.
  • Sells: the router swaps your tokens to ETH, pays 0.25% of the ETH to the fee recipient (command PAY_PORTION), then sends the rest to you.

The fee recipient is the buyback contract. You can see the fee as its own line in every swap transaction on the explorer. Example ↗

Swap fees are paid to the SmartBuyback contract. Its ETH can do exactly one thing: buy $SMART and send it to 0x000000000000000000000000000000000000dEaD.

Rules written in the code

  • No owner. Nobody can change it, pause it or upgrade it.
  • No withdraw. There is no function that sends ETH anywhere except into a buy.
  • Automatic. When a fee arrives and at least 0.001 ETH is waiting, the contract buys and burns right inside that swap.
  • Anyone can trigger it. buyback() is public; the Token page has a Burn now button.
  • Capped. One buyback spends at most 0.5 ETH, so big balances are burned in steps.

Where it buys

While $SMART is on its Pons bonding curve, the contract buys on the curve and names the burn address as the recipient. After graduation the curve refuses, and the contract buys in the Uniswap v4 pool Pons creates (ETH / $SMART, Pons graduation hook), then sends the tokens to the burn address.

Is it safe to swap through Smart?

Your wallet sends the swap to the official Uniswap Universal Router; funds never pass through a Smart contract. Every swap is simulated first and carries a minimum. The hook itself is the real risk: check its permissions on the pool page.

What does Smart charge?

0.25% of each swap in ETH, in the same transaction. All of it buys back and burns $SMART.

Why does selling need two approvals?

Uniswap's router pulls tokens through Permit2: one approval for the token, one for the router. Only the first time per token.

Which pools can I trade?

Any Uniswap v4 pool on Robinhood Chain paired with ETH. USDG and stock-token pairs are on the roadmap.

Who can take the buyback ETH?

Nobody. The buyback contract has no owner and no withdraw function.

Is this investment advice?

No. Smart shows public on-chain data and gives you a way to trade; the decision is yours.
